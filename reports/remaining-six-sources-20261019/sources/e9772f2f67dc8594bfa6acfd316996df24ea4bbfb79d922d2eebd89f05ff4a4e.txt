It has two editions. The Convention Edition was released on November 7, 2019, and the Retail Edition on March 13, 2020. The set contains mostly reprinted cards from earlier sets . &#91; 3 &#93; Each Convention booster also contains a playtest card which may be used in draft, but not in constructed play. The set is not Standard - legal . &#91; 4 &#93; Contents 1 Description 1.1 Booster pack contents 1.2 Marketing 1.2.1 Convention Edition 1.2.2 Retail Edition 1.2.3 Differences between print runs 2 Notable cards in the Retail Edition 2.1 High monetary value reprints 2.2 Memes 3 Gallery 4 References Description Mystery Booster cards with the white planeswalker symbol in the bottom left corner The idea of the Mystery Booster is that it is a product that you don't know what it is until you open it up; the mystery is a feature of the product. &#91; 5 &#93; The main set contains 1,694 cards of equal rarity. &#91; 6 &#93; &#91; 7 &#93; However, 30 cards have the mythic rare colored expansion symbol, 152 rare , 452 uncommon and 1060 common . The reprints are from throughout the game's history, going as far back as Mirage . &#91; 2 &#93; There are also cards from supplemental sets . In contrast to regular Chaos limited , which has many set-based synergy cards that don't work in that environment, this curated set maintains the chaos feel but it lets decks be functional, good, and fun to play. The cards have a white planeswalker stamp in the bottom left, meaning that these are fresh prints designed to look like the original cards. Apart from the added planeswalker symbol, the reprints are "straight pick-ups", meaning that Wizards of the Coast used the original card file to print them (including the original expansion codes). That is a different thing than printing a new card, with updated rules text, which is how they normally make cards. &#91; 8 &#93; &#91; 9 &#93; Mystery Boosters contain 15 cards. There are two versions, one for GPs / conventions and one for local WPN stores . &#91; 10 &#93; Each Convention pack has 2 commons / uncommons of each color, 1 multicolored common/uncommon, 1 common/uncommon artifact / land , 1 special slot, 1 rare / mythic rare with the M15 card frame and one pre-M15 card in its original frame. &#91; 11 &#93; Each of the 14 boosters slots from the main set of 1,694 cards has its own print sheet of 121 possible cards. Booster pack contents 12 commons or uncommons 2 cards of each color 1 multicolored card 1 artifact or land card 1 pre-M15 card 1 post-M15 rare or mythic card 1 playtest cards (convention edition only) or foil card pulled from a curated list (retail edition only) Marketing The Mystery Booster was first teased at WeeklyMTG by Blake Rasmussen on October 10, 2019. &#91; 12 &#93; &#91; 13 &#93; More info was revealed on November 7 at MagicFest Richmond &#91; 14 &#93; Convention Edition Main article&#58; Mystery Booster/Playtest cards The convention packs contain a pretend " playtest card " in the special slot that seems more like part of an Un-set . Gavin Verhey described this as "Chaos Draft meets Future Sight ". &#91; 11 &#93; These 121 so-called test cards feature black and white card faces that look like they have been stickered on cards with a regular card frame of the appropriate color. Playtest cards are not meant for Constructed play, but may be used in Chaos Draft . There is a total of 121 playtest cards (one print sheet &#91; 15 &#93; ). Playtest cards don't have a designated rarity. In August 2021 a highly limited amount of Convention Edition boosters was distributed exclusively to WPN stores, to accommodate a welcome-back experience after the COVID-19 pandemic. &#91; 16 &#93; In this edition, 2 playtest cards received new art. In addition, 24 reprints with art by Noah Bradley and Terese Nielsen were removed, while 24 other cards were doubled in frequency of appearance. &#91; 17 &#93; This printing of the playtest cards can be differentiated from the first print run since the new ones lack the planeswalker symbol in the left lower corner. Furthermore, Selesnya Guildmage and Nucklavee were replaced by Goblin Trenches and Prophetic Bolt respectively. Retail Edition To support local stores during the 2019–20 coronavirus pandemic , Wizards of the Coast announced a reprint of Mystery Booster and the allocation of the boosters to WPN member stores—at no charge. &#91; 18 &#93; Every single dollar spend on this printing was said to be going straight to the local stores. The Retail Edition of the Mystery Booster contains only reprinted cards. It filled the special slot with one of 121 possible foil cards, instead of playtest cards . &#91; 19 &#93; &#91; 20 &#93; These foil cards are different cards than those found in the main set. There are no never-before-done foils. &#91; 2 &#93; &#91; 21 &#93; Differences between print runs First print runs of Mystery Booster with the m15+ frames have a slightly larger planeswalker symbol than second print run cards. &#91; 22 &#93; First print pre-M15 cards have a smaller and squared outline around the planeswalker symbol, while second print run cards have a larger, rounded outline. The symbol in the multiple-second print runs is also not foil on foil cards, unlike first print run cards. The 121 playtest cards later dropped the "pick-up" symbol. Notable cards in the Retail Edition High monetary value reprints Bloom Tender Demonic Tutor Expropriate Mana Crypt Selvala, Heart of the Wilds Sen Triplets Teferi&#39;s Protection Memes Storm Crow — the mightiest card of all. &#91; 23 &#93; Hornet Sting — a heavy color break to attack Maro . &#91; 24 &#93; &#91; 25 &#93; One with Nothing — mirroring One with Death from the playtest cards list. &#91; 26 &#93; Chimney Imp — known for its relatively high mana cost compared to its abilities and power/toughness ratio.
